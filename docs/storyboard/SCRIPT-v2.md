# A Day in Dhaka — Storyboard v2 ("Mama's Tong")

Status: **approved by the user on 2026-10-02 (Checkpoint 1), with one addition: a Metro Rail scene (new Scene 4).** Supersedes the tea-cup "stroller" thread in `SCRIPT.md`. The page now has **nine scenes (0–8)**; the PLAN's "eight scenes only" is changed by the user's decision.
Method: mengto `scroll-world-storytelling` (thesis → beat ledger → style bible) and `no-ai-design-slop` (removal test).

---

## 1. Why v1 didn't connect

v1's thread was a faceless "stroller" who wants tea, gets it free, and gives one away at midnight. It is tidy, but a Dhaka resident never says *that is my day*:

- Nobody in it is someone you know. No one is in uniform, on a phone, late, or haggling.
- The "gift to a sleeping rickshaw puller" leans on pity. Most Dhaka people are office-goers, students, shopkeepers, garment workers, delivery riders. They don't see themselves in a charity scene.
- The setting is a generic "city". It misses what only Dhaka has: overlapping azaans, tangled wires, the elevated metro above the jam, "আসতেছি" said from the wrong end of the road.
- The art mixes two styles (atmospheric wides, blocky close-ups) because every scene had to be rebuilt as two different compositions.

## 2. Thesis

> **Every neighbourhood in Dhaka has a tong, and everyone's day passes through one.**

The whole site is one chai stall on one ordinary street corner, seen at eight moments across one working day. People come and go; the stall stays. A visitor from Mirpur, Old Dhaka, Uttara or Jatrabari should think: *that's my para's tong.*

**Why it connects (the relatability test every scene must pass):** can a Dhaka resident point at something and say *"ha, exactly"* within three seconds, with no explanation? If a scene needs a caption to explain itself, it fails.

**Emotional engine: the বাকির খাতা (credit notebook).** Every tong owner keeps one. In the morning a worker has no change and Mama says "পরে দিয়েন" (pay later) and writes a line. By evening the line is settled. Dhaka runs on this small trust. It replaces v1's invented "debt" with the real thing, and the midnight ending is Mama closing the notebook, not a stranger leaving a cup.

## 3. The day (anchoring in reality)

An **ordinary working day in late October, just after the rains** (flag for review: confirm fajr and sunset times for that week).
- Fajr at about 4:45 and dusk at about 5:30 PM fit the existing clock anchors. 
- Humid, still hot at noon, hazy rather than clear-sky blue. Dhaka's sky is dust-cream, not postcard blue.
- Kite weather is real after the monsoon.
- Not a holiday, not Friday. The city is going to work.

## 5. World: one set, eight lights

**The set (build once, relight eight times):** a corner tong under a faded tarpaulin awning, with a kettle on a gas burner, a glass jar of biscuits, a bench, a hanging bunch of bananas, the red notebook. Behind it: a six-storey apartment block with AC compressors and grilled balconies, mildew-streaked concrete, a hand-painted shop signboard, a thicket of black overhead wires, a rooftop water tank, a minaret in the distance.

This replaces v1's seven separate scene designs with one environment that changes with time and cast. It fixes the "inconsistent look" directly and cuts art work to a fraction.

**Camera grammar:** a single slow glide toward and past the tong in each scene (no double-exposed wide/close cross-fade). The tong is the constant; everything else moves past it.

## 7. Scene scripts (what you see, hear, read)

Text rule: Bangla first and in **spoken** register (Dhaka street speech), English subtitle ≤ 8 words. **Two registers only**, dropping v1's three:
1. **Narration** (large, once per scene): one calm line.
2. **Overheard** (small, in quotes, near the speaker): one spoken line.

### Scene 0 — The bulb
A single bare bulb on a wire in the dark, swinging a hair. It warms from orange to gold as loading finishes, swells, and the title rises out of the light.
- Title: **একটি দিন, ঢাকায়** / *A Day in Dhaka*. Prompt: **নিচে স্ক্রল করুন** / *Scroll to begin*.
- Sound (later): a kettle ticking as it heats.

### Scene 1 — 4:45 AM, "ফজরের আগে"
Mist between apartment blocks. Overhead wires dripping. A shutter rattles up. Mama strikes a match, the blue burner flame catches, the kettle goes on. The first azaan starts, then a second, a third, each a half-beat off, because that is what Dhaka sounds like. The night guard shuffles up, torch dimming.
- Narration: **আজান শেষ, চুলা জ্বলে** / *The azaan ends, the stove lights.*
- Overheard: guard: **“মামা, এক কাপ।”** / *“One cup, Mama.”*
- Recognisable: the first tea of the day belongs to whoever stayed awake. The tong opens before the city does.

### Scene 2 — 7:00 AM, "সবাই দেরি করে বেরোয়"
Light comes in warm and low. Parathas on the tawa, singara in the glass case, bananas swaying. School kids with bags bigger than their backs. A stream of garment workers walking in groups to the factory, tiffins swinging. A newspaper hawker sorts a bundle on the bench. A young woman holds out a Tk 500 note for a Tk 10 tea. Mama shakes his head: it's too early to have change.
- Narration: **সবাই দেরি করে বেরোয়** / *Everyone leaves a little late.*
- Overheard: worker: **“মামা, ভাংতি নাই।”** / *“No change, Mama.”* Mama: **“পরে দিয়েন।”** / *“Pay me later.”* (Then the first line is written in the red notebook, a close beat of the pencil.)
- Recognisable: the baki khata. A worker running for a bus with a half-eaten paratha.

### Scene 3 — 9:00 AM, "জ্যাম" (hero)
We leave the tong for the main road. Rickshaw art in full colour: magenta flowers, tin-foil tinsel, hand-painted film-poster faces. Dented buses with the helper hanging off the door banging the side: **"ফার্মগেট ফার্মগেট!"** CNG autos in their green cages. Bike riders weaving through the gap. Overhead, the elevated metro glides past the jam, empty of noise. Giant words behind and in front of vehicles are what people actually say: **আসতেছি** (on my way), **জ্যাম** (jam), **সামনে আগান** (move forward). The jam beat: everything locks, horns, a commuter on the phone says "আসতেছি" while clearly nowhere near. Then it bursts free, and the camera lifts up a concrete pier onto the elevated line (hand-off to Scene 4).
- Giant words: **আসতেছি** / *on my way* · **জ্যাম** / *jam* · **সামনে আগান** / *move up, please*
- Overheard: commuter: **“আসতেছি, পাঁচ মিনিট।”** / *“Coming, five minutes.”*
- Closing line: **৯টা বাজে, ঢাকা থামে না** / *9 o'clock, and Dhaka never stops.*
- Recognisable: the lie everyone tells, the bus helper's chant, the metro above the jam.

### Scene 4 — 9:40 AM, "ঢাকাকে উপর থেকে দেখা" (new: Metro Rail)

The only quiet minute of the morning. Scene 3 ends with the road still locked; the camera lifts up a concrete pier to the elevated line and the street noise drops away. A clean white train with a red-green stripe (see Review item 6) glides across the viaduct, right to left, above the same jam. Then we move *into* the window: the carriage window is the frame, and the city slides past behind it in layers (roofs, water tanks, billboards, the jam far below). Inside: seats, handrails, a route-map strip, the yellow line at the platform edge. This is the diegetic replacement for v1's wide/close cross-fade: the window *is* the push-in.

**Emotions, in order (each is a real, unsentimental moment):**

| Beat | Feeling | What you see | Overheard / sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The glide | **Hush, relief** | The roar of the street ducks to a soft rail hum. Below, the buses are still stuck. | the train's low hum; a two-note chime |
| 2. The boy | **Wonder** | A boy of about 8, nose flat on the glass, father's hand on his shoulder. Directly below: a bus like the ones he has sat in for two hours on other days. | **“বাবা, দেখো! আমরা বাসের উপরে!”** / *“Baba, look! We're above the buses!”* |
| 3. The commuter | **Vindication, a small laugh** | The same commuter from Scene 3, now seated, AC on his neck, thumbing a message. | **“এইবার সত্যি আসতেছি।”** / *“This time I'm really coming.”* (the lie from the jam becomes true) |
| 4. The mother | **Tenderness** | An elderly mother in a white sari on her first ride, gripping the pole, her son's hand under her elbow. She looks at the skyline, says nothing, then smiles. | **“ওরে বাবা, কত উঁচু!”** / *“Oh my, how high!”* |
| 5. The worker | **Rest, with a thread** | The garment worker from Scene 2 sits by the window with her eyes closed: the first quiet of her day. She has broken her big note at the station counter; a Tk 10 note is folded into her lanyard pouch, kept apart from the rest: the morning's debt. | silence |
| 6. Everyone looks down | **Belonging, quiet pride** | As the train crosses the worst stretch of the jam, the whole carriage turns to the window together. Nobody speaks. | the announcement: **পরবর্তী স্টেশন: ফার্মগেট** / *Next station: Farmgate.* |

- Narration (large, once): **ঢাকাকে উপর থেকে দেখা** / *Seeing Dhaka from above.*
- **Exception to the one-overheard-line rule:** this scene may carry three overheard lines (beats 2, 3, 4), each at most 6 English words and shown one at a time as you scroll. It is the emotional scene. Beats 5 and 6 stay silent, which is what lets the others land.
- Motion: sticky stage. 0–0.30 the train crosses the viaduct (lateral track); 0.30–0.40 the push through the window; 0.40–0.85 interior beats 2–6 tied to scroll, with the window layers sliding at different speeds; 0.85–1.0 doors open, light floods in, hand-off to Scene 5.
- Sound (ships later): roar ducks to rail hum; chime; announcement; the soft breath of the doors.
- Recognisable: getting a seat, AC on a hot morning, seeing your own city from above for the first time, a parent on a first ride, the announcement chime. Pride without a flag-wave: the city built one thing that moves above its own traffic.
- Transition: doors open, the light goes white-hot, and the carriage dissolves into the bleached noon of Scene 5.

### Scene 5 — 1:00 PM, "ছায়াটাই এখন দামি"
The sky goes white. Shadows shrink under the awning. Shutters half-down. A rickshaw puller asleep across his own seat, feet up on the handlebar, a gamchha over his face. Mama fans himself with a tali. The ceiling fan above the stall slows... and stops. The whole street sighs at once.
- Narration: **রোদ যত কড়া, ছায়া তত দামি** / *The harsher the sun, the more precious the shade.* (kept from v1)
- Overheard: someone off-screen: **“আবার কারেন্ট গেল!”** / *“Power's gone again!”*
- Recognisable: the puller's nap, the fan dying, lemon tea, the hand fan.

### Scene 6 — 4:30 PM, "ছাদ আমাদের উঠান"
Up to the rooftop. A sea of water tanks, clotheslines, satellite dishes, other roofs close enough to shout across. Kites over the haze, pigeons circling a coop. One kid's string cuts another's: **"ভো-কাট্টা!"** The cut kite floats down across the lane, and every kid on every roof and street runs after it.
- Narration: **ছাদ আমাদের উঠান** / *The rooftop is our courtyard.*
- Overheard: kid: **“ভো-কাট্টা!”** / *“Vo-kaatta!”* (the kite-cut cry)
- Recognisable: Dhaka has almost no yards, so the roof is the playground. The cut-kite chase.

### Scene 7 — 7:30 PM, "বাড়ি ফেরার আগে, একটু আড্ডা"
The bulbs come on. Signboards, fairy lights, a fuchka cart with a glowing tamarind-water jar, jhalmuri men, the second jam headed home, headlights smearing across wet road. At the tong, office-goers with lanyards stand around with cha and biscuit. A swarm of mosquitoes circles the bulb. The garment worker from the morning pushes a Tk 10 note across.
- Narration: **বাড়ি ফেরার আগে, একটু আড্ডা** / *A little adda before home.*
- Overheard: worker: **“মামা, সকালেরটা নেন।”** / *“Mama, here's this morning's.”* Mama: **“কাটলাম।”** / *“Struck it off.”* (The notebook line is crossed through.)
- Recognisable: the adda, the second rush, the fuchka cart, the debt quietly settled.

### Scene 8 — 11:45 PM, "ঘুমায় শহর"
The street empties. Shutters down. A dog asleep by the drain. One window still lit. Mama counts the day's coins into a tin, closes the red notebook, pulls the shutter. Alone under the one bulb he dials. A quiet voice. The bulb goes off; the screen of the phone is the last light, then the circle shrinks to the dot that opened the site.
- Narration: **ঘুমায় শহর, স্বপ্ন জাগে** / *The city sleeps, the dreams stay awake.* (kept from v1)
- Overheard: Mama, on the phone: **“হ্যাঁ, খাইছি। তুমি খাইছো?”** / *“Yes, I ate. Have you?”*
- Closing line after credits: **কাল আবার।** / *Again tomorrow.* above the restart button.
- Recognisable: "খাইছো?" is how a Bengali says "I love you". Most of Dhaka came from somewhere else, and this is the call home.

## 8. Style bible

- **Mood:** worn, warm, wry.
- **World metaphor:** one street corner. The city passes through it.
- **Palette (6 names, dominant + accent):**
  | Name | Hex | Role |
  |---|---|---|
  | Dust Haze | `#E9DFC8` | dominant day field (Dhaka's sky is cream, not blue) |
  | Mildew Concrete | `#8A8F7E` | buildings, stained by monsoon |
  | Tarpaulin Blue | `#2F5D8C` | the awning, blue tarps everywhere |
  | Rickshaw Magenta | `#D6336C` | scene 3's loudest colour, painted panels |
  | Tong Amber | `#F2A93B` | **the one accent**: bulb, tea, flame |
  | Night Ink | `#11151F` | dominant night field |
- **Typography:** Noto Serif Bengali for narration; Baloo Da 2 only for speech in quotes and the giant words (it reads as sign-painter lettering); a plain grotesk for English and the clock. No new fonts.
- **Material language:** printed. A little riso-style overprint, paper grain, slightly misregistered shapes. Closer to a film-poster or tea-stall calendar than a vector icon set.
- **Motion grammar:** lateral glide to the tong; one pinned pan in Scene 3; slow in-out in calm scenes, snappy in 3 and 6.
- **Pacing:** quiet, soft, loud, hot, free, electric, quiet.
- **Exclusions (three clichés to avoid):**
  1. Pity framing: no beggars, no slum shots, no "poor but happy" glow. The cast is the working middle of the city.
  2. Postcard Dhaka: no clean blue sky, no lone nouka at sunset, no rickshaw as the only symbol.
  3. SaaS polish: no glass cards, aurora gradients, floating pills. The only "UI" is a printed caption and the clock.
